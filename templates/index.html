Flood extent: DEM threshold / bathtub approach

Screening equation

Fᵢ = 1 if H ≥ zᵢ; otherwise Fᵢ = 0 hᵢ = max(H − zᵢ, 0)

H is the user-selected water-surface elevation, zᵢ is DEM ground elevation, and hᵢ is estimated depth. The optional connected-waterway setting restricts the flood mask to cells connected to mapped waterways when a valid seed intersection is found. These equations describe the simplified screening method in this WebGIS; they are not a full hydraulic simulation.

  • U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). Flood Inundation Mapping Science. Official source.
  • Esri. Flood Simulation in ArcGIS Pro (technical paper). The ArcGIS simulation describes a more complete flow model using continuity and momentum equations; this WebGIS does not implement that full model. Technical paper (PDF).
  • Gesch, D. B. (2018). Best Practices for Elevation-Based Assessments of Sea-Level Rise and Coastal Flooding Exposure. Frontiers in Earth Science, 6, 230. https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2018.00230.
Agricultural damage equation
D = H × P × Σᵢ[aᵢ × f(hᵢ,dᵢ)]

This equation structure is Equation (1), Section 3.2, in Budhathoki, A., Tanaka, T., & Tachikawa, Y. (2024). “Developing flood risk curves of agricultural economic damage under climate change in the Lower Chao Phraya River Basin, Thailand.” Journal of Flood Risk Management, 17(4), e13031.

https://doi.org/10.1111/jfr3.13031

Implementation note: The current f(h,d) function in this WebGIS is a provisional example. It is not the paper’s calibrated rice fragility curve and has not been validated for every crop type. The article studies rice in the Lower Chao Phraya River Basin.
